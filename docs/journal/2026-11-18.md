## Outcome

## Reasoning

The compact formulas assumed that readers already knew polynomial evaluation,
fixed-width bit reversal and modular inverses. Explain each operation before
combining them. A four-point example makes the stored ordering visible without
requiring a reader to follow a 65536-element calculation.

Keep the production formulas at N=65536 and label the small example explicitly.
Put the cancellation proof in optional notes, so readers can follow the
calculation before studying why it works.

### CKKS Fourier-extension functional bootstrapping

Reviewed the tagged OpenFHE 1.6.1 implementation and the EUROCRYPT 2026
abstract. In the slots-to-coefficients-first path, a complex exponential
cancels integer overflows from modulus raising. The Fourier polynomial then
evaluates a function of each real input slot. The single-call API accepts
coefficients for ReLU as well as smooth functions. A finite series rounds
the corner of hard ReLU.

Added [the implementation study](../ckks-fourier-functional-bootstrap.md)
to Research. It records APIs, admissible functions, ReLU coefficients and
the local CPU comparison against ordinary bootstrapping. The comparison
times whole calls and excludes context setup, key generation and decryption.

On a single-threaded M1 Pro with 1,024 slots, all six modes left eight
effective levels. The median ordinary bootstrap was 10.76 s. The three
official smooth-function examples took 13.46–17.59 s, while 45- and
129-degree ReLU took 14.24 s and 20.54 s. The corresponding ReLU maximum
absolute errors were 0.0571 and 0.0127 over the tested grid, matching the
plaintext Fourier evaluation at the displayed precision. These are
diagnostic timings under one parameter set, with three measured samples
per mode and without an independent security estimate.
All 27 documentation tests passed, and the site build checked 42 pages.

Next research comparison, if measuring the savings from fusion, is ordinary
bootstrapping followed by a separate evaluator for the same function at
matched error and output level count.

### Hard ReLU degree sweep

Extended the functional bootstrap comparison to eight Fourier degrees from
9 to 325 under the same single-threaded OpenFHE 1.6.1 configuration.
Reused the three-sample results for degrees 45 and 129. Each new degree
used one warm-up and two measured calls. Every result retained eight
effective output levels.

The depth budget rose from 19 to 24. Degrees 257 and 325 both used
24 levels and took 25.59 s and 29.11 s, respectively. The ciphertext
results matched the same-grid plaintext error summaries to within 1e-7.
Deriving the exact plaintext Fourier tail and checking a 32,769-point grid
showed that the original 1,024 slots miss the error peak at the upper
boundary. Degree 129 has a slot maximum of 0.01266 and an interval
supremum of 0.02513. With this extension on [-8, 8), degree 325 is the
first to put the approximation supremum below 0.01. The ciphertext
numerical error is a separate contribution.

Added the sweep, raw timings, exact error formula and depth thresholds to
[the implementation study](../ckks-fourier-functional-bootstrap.md). A
follow-up at the same error budget could measure how changing the extension
outside the target interval affects the required degree and latency.
